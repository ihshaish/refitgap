Shared-rule cost: one rule across periods
=========================================

Aim
---
The mismatch R compares a final model's attainable level, where each period has its own best
rule, with the level of one deployed rule. Part of R may be the price of serving several periods
with one rule at all. We split R = C + M: C is the attainable level under separate rules minus
that under the best single rule (the shared-rule cost), and M is the distance of the deployed
rule from the best single rule. Two questions are kept apart: what one rule costs in principle,
and which fitted policy does better with the labels at hand. The quantities apply only where one
shared rule is required; per-period rules are legitimate wherever period identity is available.

Design
------
Stored scores only; no training. The 200 runs of the method comparison (sequential training,
replay, extra updates on current data, LwF, EWC; four streams; seeds 0 to 9); MultiEURLEX with
sequential training, replay and LwF (10 seeds each); the ASRS paired refit with sequential
training and replay (seeds 0 to 9). The vocabulary pair is the principal controlled stream. On
the reweighted pair the cost is expected to be positive by construction (the prior steps at
period 4), so only differences between arms are read there. On natural streams a method is
compared with sequential training on the same stream.

Analysis
--------
The shared rule is the exact maximiser of the mean over periods of per-label F1. Every term is
cross-fitted on the method comparison's 200 split-half partitions. A contrast Delta C is sequential
training minus the method, paired by seed (ten pairs), control-differenced on the two pairs.
Each contrast: mean, 95% paired t interval (9 df), TOST at a margin of 0.005, exact sign-flip
check (1024 patterns), and the method comparison's outcomes (equivalent, material, small
non-zero, unresolved). The margin stays at 0.005.

First specification. Shared rule fitted at matched total budget (on all pooled fitting halves,
each separate rule seeing its own period's share); periods 2 to 7; Delta C per arm; Holm within each
arm over streams. It did not say whether "over streams" meant control-differenced pairs or
single streams, so an arm's outcome must agree across five sets of tests: (1) the two pairs;
(2) the four streams of the method comparison; (3) those four and MultiEURLEX; (4) the two pairs
and the natural ASRS stream; (5) the two pairs, the ASRS stream and MultiEURLEX. Where the five
disagree, the arm is reported as dependent on the specification.

All-period variant. Budget matched per fit (the shared rule sees about as many records as each
separate rule); all eight periods, the current one included at equal weight; the vocabulary
pair only. One family of four, Holm across them: Delta C for LwF, for EWC and for replay, and replay
minus extra updates on current data in Delta C. Everything else is unadjusted and decides nothing:
M, R, the attainable gap under a shared rule, the matched total budget, periods 1 to 7 and 2 to
7, the inherited thresholds, the reweighted pair and the natural streams.

Also planned. A simulation with eight periods and known optima (four scenarios, three sizes)
measures the bias of the per-fit estimate; if its reach 2b exceeds 0.0025 every reading of the
family of four is qualified (it came to 0.0009). Per-fit budgets of 100, 250, 500 and 1000
records are descriptive, without extrapolation. Per arm and stream (15 cells, the reweighted
pair left out), the per-fit cost less the simulated bias is called equivalent to zero within
0.005 only if its TOST passes after Holm across the 15. At matched total budget each cell reads
"shared wins" or "separate wins" when the 95% interval excludes zero, otherwise equivalent
within 0.005 or unresolved; a negative fitted cost is never called a negative cost in principle.
The paper's per-period change in mismatch is not split into C and M.

Predictions
-----------
None. An expectation without a falsifier: on the vocabulary pair the control-differenced cost
is about zero for every arm, and each Delta C is equivalent or unresolved. Its basis is an earlier
reading on sequential ASRS runs (one shared rule 0.7185, per-period rules 0.7178, periods 2 to
7, matched total budget).

Date
----
Written 29 Oct 2026, before any C or M of a stored run was computed; only synthetic numbers had
been seen. It was specified after the method comparison's results were known and reuses that
comparison's seeds, so it is an extension on inspected runs.

Departures
----------
1. The plan of 29 Oct 2026 made the all-period variant principal. The same day, after its
   outcomes were seen, we took the first specification to govern and report the variant beside
   it. The five sets of tests were written after the outcomes were seen, with some
   vocabulary-pair cells already printed.
2. The variant changes budget, period range and family together. EWC's outcome differs between
   the two and is reported as dependent on the specification. For EWC's small non-zero Delta C the
   planned words "below the margin" are withdrawn: the estimate is below 0.005, but the effect
   is not shown to lie below it (one-sided p 0.22).
3. "Positive by construction" on the reweighted pair is an expectation, not a certainty (the
   observed per-fit cost under sequential training is 0.0132).
