Analysis plan: replay against sequential training after refit (DistilBERT, paired)
==================================================================================

Aim
---
To measure how much of replay's lead over sequential training remains for a pretrained
transformer fine-tuned end to end once both models refit one threshold per label. This carries
the BiGRU paired refit (bigru-paired-refit.txt) to DistilBERT on two streams. It tests no
repair.

Design
------
ASRS primary: 8 periods, 9 labels, 831 validation records, 2069 to 2104 test records per period,
7479 then 8279 to 8406 training records per period, replay budget 3342.
MultiEURLEX: 8 periods, 12 labels, 450 validation records, 1116 to 1131 test records per period,
4052 then 4470 to 4518 training records per period, replay budget 1801.
About 38% of ASRS records and all MultiEURLEX records in a sampled batch exceed 256 subword
tokens and are truncated.
Arms: sequential training (each period trains on its own records) and replay (each period also
trains on a reservoir buffer of 5% of the training pool, replayed in full). Both arms of a seed
load the same initialisation and train the same first period, so they share theta1 on one device.
Recipe, as in the DistilBERT decomposition: distilbert-base-uncased, AdamW at 2e-5, linear
warm-up over 10% of each period's updates, batch 32, 256 subword tokens, 3 epochs per period;
thresholds theta1 fitted once on a 19-point grid (0.05 to 0.95) on the first-period validation
split and then inherited. Seeds 0 to 5. New runs: MultiEURLEX sequential (6), MultiEURLEX replay
(6), ASRS replay (6). The ASRS sequential members were to be the six stored runs of the
DistilBERT decomposition, whose backward results we had already seen; the ASRS pairs were to be
labelled accordingly. All runs are reported, with no selection among seeds. No finite-label
refit, no extra updates on current data, no pooling with the BiGRU results.

Repeatability rule
------------------
A repeat (same seed and device, second process) or a pairing check passes when both hold:
(a) the largest absolute difference in the first-period model's scores is at most 0.0122, the
    median between-seed standard deviation of those scores over the six stored initialisations;
(b) the difference in the first-period model's mean macro-F1 at theta1 over the eight periods is at
    most 0.0013, which is 0.2 of that quantity's between-seed standard deviation (0.0066) and
    about a quarter of the 0.005 margin.
The first period of stored ASRS seed 3 is trained twice before any other run; the same rule
checks the pairing within each MultiEURLEX seed and the reuse of each stored ASRS sequential run
against the first period of its new replay partner. A stored run that fails is replaced by a new
sequential run on the device of its partner, decided before any replay-minus-sequential quantity
is computed. The constants come from the stored decomposition scores and from no result of this
plan. The rule was written after a repeat difference of 0.0029 had been seen on an earlier run,
where the tolerance had been 1e-3.

Analysis
--------
Per stream, pair and historical period 2 to 7 (fixed before the runs), on the final model:
U_m, macro-F1 of arm m at theta1 on the whole period; V_m, attainable macro-F1 after refit, by exact
split-half selection over 200 partitions shared within a pair; G_U = U_replay - U_seq, the
inherited lead; G_V = V_replay - V_seq, the refitted lead; Delta = G_U - G_V. Each is averaged over
periods within a pair, then over the 6 pairs.
Per stream the test family is {G_U, G_V}: paired t, TOST at the 0.005 margin, Holm within the
family, and an exact two-sided sign-flip test that overrides (minimum p 2/64 = 0.031 at 6
pairs). Each lead is classed as equivalent, material, small non-zero or unresolved. Beside each
we print the percentile bootstrap over pairs (20000 resamples) and the count of positive pairs.
Delta with its bootstrap interval is descriptive. The two streams are separate families.
Secondary, descriptive: the forward comparison by lag for each stream and arm (shortfall, its
attainable part and the change in mismatch), with the stale model's own-period rule; lags 1 to 5
reported, lags 6 and 7 printed with the training-volume confound stated; bootstrap over seeds;
the share formed only where the shortfall's interval excludes zero.

Predictions
-----------
Informed by the BiGRU paired results (ASRS G_V +0.0034 [0.0019, 0.0049], G_U +0.0086;
MultiEURLEX G_V +0.0243, G_U +0.0305) and the DistilBERT decomposition (deficit +0.0161,
attainable gap -0.0015, change in mismatch +0.0176).
1. ASRS: G_V equivalent within 0.005; G_U positive, small non-zero or material.
2. MultiEURLEX: G_V material and positive; G_U material and positive; Delta positive.
3. Forward comparison, both streams: the attainable part is at least 0.8 of the shortfall at
   every lag from 1 to 5 where a share is formed.
Wording fixed for G_V, per stream: equivalent, "equivalent to zero within 0.005 once both
refitted"; material and positive, "kept a lead beyond 0.005"; small non-zero, "kept a lead below
0.005 that the interval separates from zero"; negative, the same with "trailed"; unresolved,
"unresolved relative to 0.005 at 6 pairs". We write that the inherited lead is all mismatch
only when G_U is material or small non-zero and G_V is equivalent.

Date
----
9 November 2026, before any run of this plan: nothing had been trained, scored or launched.
Stops are technical only (repeatability, memory, a failed validation, a third attempt) and none
depends on a difference between arms. A seed that cannot complete is reported as missing.

Departures
----------
An earlier DistilBERT repair design, planned on 1 November, stopped at its repeatability check
(0.0029 against its tolerance of 1e-3); that stop says nothing about any prediction.
The stored ASRS sequential runs did not pass the reuse check, so every ASRS pair was retrained
from new runs, and all runs moved to one hardware cohort. Both changes, and the added comparison
with extra updates on current data, are in distilbert-matched-updates.txt.
